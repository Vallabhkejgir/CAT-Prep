## 1. 30-Second Concept & Formula Blueprint

### 1.1 Linear Systems & Integer Diophantine Equations
- **Consistency of $a_1 x + b_1 y = c_1$ and $a_2 x + b_2 y = c_2$**:
  - **Unique Solution** (Intersecting lines): $\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}$
  - **No Solution** (Parallel distinct lines): $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}$
  - **Infinitely Many Solutions** (Coincident lines): $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$
- **Linear Diophantine Equation $ax + by = c$ ($a, b > 0, \gcd(a,b) = 1$)**:
  - Once one integer solution $(x_0, y_0)$ is found, all integer solutions are generated by shifting in steps of $b$ and $a$:
    $$x = x_0 + kb, \quad y = y_0 - ka \quad (k \in \mathbb{Z})$$
  - **Number of non-negative integer solutions** ($x \ge 0, y \ge 0$): Either $\left\lfloor \frac{c}{ab} \right\rfloor$ or $\left\lfloor \frac{c}{ab} \right\rfloor + 1$.
  - **Frobenius Coin / Postage Stamp Theorem**: For coprime denominations $a, b > 1$:
    - Largest amount that **cannot** be formed using non-negative integers $x, y \ge 0$ is $ab - a - b$.
    - Largest amount that **cannot** be formed using positive integers $x, y \ge 1$ is $ab$.
    - Total number of unattainable non-negative values is $\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}{2}$.

### 1.2 Quadratic & Higher-Degree Polynomials
- **Vieta's Formulas for $P(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_0 = 0$**:
  $$\sum r_i = -\frac{a_{n-1}}{a_n}, \quad \sum_{i < j} r_i r_j = +\frac{a_{n-2}}{a_n}, \quad \prod_{i=1}^{n} r_i = (-1)^n \frac{a_0}{a_n}$$
- **Useful Root Power Sums** (for quadratic roots $\alpha, \beta$ with $S = \alpha + \beta, P = \alpha\beta$):
  - $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = S^2 - 2P$
  - $|\alpha - \beta| = \sqrt{S^2 - 4P} = \frac{\sqrt{\Delta}}{|a|}$
  - $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = S^3 - 3SP$
- **Discriminant & Nature of Roots ($ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, $a \neq 0$)**:
  - $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac > 0$: Two distinct real roots (rational iff $a, b, c \in \mathbb{Q}$ and $\Delta$ is a perfect square).
  - $\Delta = 0$: Real and equal roots (graph touches x-axis at $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$).
  - $\Delta < 0$: Complex conjugate roots; $ax^2 + bx + c$ has the **same sign as $a$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$**:
    $$\forall x \in \mathbb{R},\; ax^2 + bx + c > 0 \iff a > 0 \text{ and } \Delta < 0$$
- **Location of Roots Relative to a Number $k$**:
  - $k$ lies **strictly between** the roots ($\alpha < k < \beta$): $a \cdot f(k) < 0$ (sufficient; implies $\Delta > 0$ automatically).
  - Both roots $> k$: $\Delta \ge 0$, $a \cdot f(k) > 0$, and $-\frac{b}{2a} > k$.
  - Both roots $\in (k_1, k_2)$: $\Delta \ge 0$, $a \cdot f(k_1) > 0$, $a \cdot f(k_2) > 0$, and $k_1 < -\frac{b}{2a} < k_2$.
- **Remainder & Factor Theorems**:
  - Remainder when $P(x)$ is divided by $(x - a)$ is $P(a)$.
  - Sum of all coefficients of $P(x)$ is $P(1)$; constant term is $P(0)$; sum of even-degree coefficients is $\frac{P(1)+P(-1)}{2}$.

### 1.3 Inequalities & Modulus (Absolute Value)
- **Wavy Curve (Method of Intervals)**:
  1. Factor into $\frac{\prod (x - r_i)^{m_i}}{\prod (x - p_j)^{n_j}} \gtrless 0$ with all leading coefficients of $x$ positive.
  2. Start with $+$ to the right of the largest critical point.
  3. Moving left across a critical point $c$:
     - **Odd multiplicity** ($m_i$ odd) $\implies$ Sign **flips**.
     - **Even multiplicity** ($m_i$ even) $\implies$ Sign **stays the same** (check explicitly whether $x = c$ satisfies $\ge / \le$ or makes denominator zero!).
- **Modulus Identities & Triangle Inequalities**:
  - $|x| < a \iff -a < x < a \quad (a > 0)$
  - $|f(x)| + |g(x)| = |f(x) + g(x)| \iff f(x) \cdot g(x) \ge 0$ (same sign or zero)
  - $|f(x)| + |g(x)| = |f(x) - g(x)| \iff f(x) \cdot g(x) \le 0$ (opposite signs or zero)
  - $|f(x)| - |g(x)| = |f(x) - g(x)| \iff g(x)[f(x) - g(x)] \ge 0$
- **Sum of Absolute Deviations $g(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x - a_i|$ ($a_1 \le a_2 \le \dots \le a_n$)**:
  - Minimum occurs at the **Median**:
    - If $n = 2m + 1$ (odd): Unique minimum at $x = a_{m+1}$.
    - If $n = 2m$ (even): Constant minimum plateau for all $x \in [a_m, a_{m+1}]$.
  - Value of minimum $= (a_n - a_1) + (a_{n-1} - a_2) + \dots$ (pair outermost points inward).
- **Diamond / Rhombus Graph $|x - h| + |y - k| = r$**:
  - Encloses a rotated square of side $r\sqrt{2}$ and **Area $= 2r^2$**.
  - Number of integer lattice points $(x, y)$ strictly on the boundary $= 4r$ (for $r \in \mathbb{Z}^+$); inside and on boundary $= 2r^2 + 2r + 1$.

## 3. Canonical CAT Archetypes & Solved Exemplars

### Archetype 1: Wavy Curve with Even Multiplicity & Denominator Trap
> **Problem**: Find the number of integer values of $x \in [-6, 6]$ that satisfy $\frac{(x - 2)^4 (x + 1)^3 (x - 4)}{(x + 3)^2 (x - 1)} \le 0$.

- **First-Principles Method**:
  1. **Domain restriction**: $x \neq -3$ and $x \neq 1$.
  2. **Zero roots (numerator $= 0$)**: $x = 2, -1, 4$ are included in the solution set.
  3. **Sign-changing (odd-power) critical points**: $x \in \{-1, 1, 4\}$. (Even-power factors $(x-2)^4$ and $(x+3)^2$ are $\ge 0$ everywhere on the valid domain.)
  4. Sign chart on odd-power points $\{-1, 1, 4\}$: $(-\infty, -1] \cup (1, 4]$.
  5. Don't forget $x = 2$ (already inside $(1, 4]$) and exclude $x = -3$ (which lies inside $(-\infty, -1]$!).
  6. Valid integers in $[-6, 6]$:
     - From $[-6, -1]$ excluding $-3$: $\{-6, -5, -4, -2, -1\}$ (5 integers).
     - From $(1, 4]$: $\{2, 3, 4\}$ (3 integers).
     - Total $= 5 + 3 = \mathbf{8\text{ integers}}$.

- **30-Second Exam-Hall Speed Shortcut**:
  - Strip even powers immediately, writing down two sticky notes: **Include $\{2\}$**, **Exclude $\{-3\}$**.
  - Solve $\frac{(x+1)(x-4)}{x-1} \le 0 \implies (-\infty, -1] \cup (1, 4]$, then apply the two sticky notes!

### Archetype 2: Factoring 2-Variable Diophantine Equations ($xy + ax + by = c$)
> **Problem**: How many ordered pairs of positive integers $(x, y)$ satisfy $xy - 3x + 4y = 29$?

- **First-Principles Method**:
  1. Add $(-3)(4) = -12$ to both sides to complete the rectangle (Simon's Favorite Factoring Trick):
     $$xy - 3x + 4y - 12 = 29 - 12 \implies (x + 4)(y - 3) = 17$$
  2. Since $x, y \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, $x + 4 \ge 5$, so $x + 4$ must be a positive divisor of $17$ that is $\ge 5$.
  3. The only such divisor of prime $17$ is $17$ itself:
     $$x + 4 = 17 \implies x = 13, \quad y - 3 = 1 \implies y = 4$$
  4. Unique solution $(13, 4) \implies \mathbf{1\text{ pair}}$.

- **30-Second Exam-Hall Speed Shortcut**:
  - Isolate $x$: $x = \frac{29 - 4y}{y - 3} = \frac{17 - 4(y - 3)}{y - 3} = -4 + \frac{17}{y - 3}$.
  - For $x \ge 1$, $y - 3$ must divide $17$ positively and $\frac{17}{y-3} \ge 5 \implies y - 3 = 1 \implies \mathbf{1\text{ solution}}$.

### Archetype 3: Nested Modulus Equation Counting
> **Problem**: Find the number of real roots of the equation $\big| |x - 3| - 4 \big| = 2$.

- **First-Principles Method**:
  1. Peel outer modulus: $|x - 3| - 4 = \pm 2 \implies |x - 3| = 6$ or $|x - 3| = 2$.
  2. Since both $6 > 0$ and $2 > 0$, each yields $2$ distinct real roots:
     - $x - 3 = \pm 6 \implies x \in \{9, -3\}$
     - $x - 3 = \pm 2 \implies x \in \{5, 1\}$
  3. Total $= \mathbf{4\text{ real roots}}$ (and by symmetry around $x = 3$, the sum of all roots is $4 \times 3 = 12$).

- **30-Second Exam-Hall Speed Shortcut (Graphical W-Curve)**:
  - $|x - 3|$ is a V-graph with vertex at $(3, 0)$.
  - Shifting down by $4$ and reflecting below the x-axis creates a **W-graph** with twin bottom vertices at $y = 0$ and central peak at $(3, 4)$.
  - Any horizontal line $y = c$ with $0 < c < 4$ intersects the W-graph in **4 points**; at $c = 0$ or $c = 4$, it intersects in **3 points**; for $c > 4$, **2 points**. Since $c = 2 \in (0, 4) \implies \mathbf{4\text{ roots}}$.

## 4. Boundary Conditions, Domain Traps & TITA Pitfalls

1. **Cross-Multiplying Inequalities with Variable Denominators**:
   - **NEVER** cross-multiply $\frac{x+2}{x-3} < 2$ to get $x + 2 < 2(x - 3)$ because $(x - 3)$ can be negative! Always bring all terms to one side: $\frac{x+2 - 2(x-3)}{x-3} < 0 \iff \frac{8 - x}{x - 3} < 0$.
2. **Degenerate Leading Coefficient Trap ($a = 0$)**:
   - If a problem says "Find all values of $m$ for which the inequality $mx^2 - 4mx + 5 > 0$ holds for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$" (without calling it a "quadratic" equation), $m = 0$ gives $5 > 0$ which is **TRUE**! Always check $a = 0$ separately.
3. **Real Roots Condition Before Vieta's Sum of Squares**:
   - If asked to find $k$ such that $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 10$ for $x^2 - 2x + k = 0$, Vieta gives $4 - 2k = 10 \implies k = -3$ ($\Delta = 4 - 4(-3) = 16 > 0$, valid). However, if $x^2 - 2x + k = 0$ had given $k = 5$, $\Delta = 4 - 20 < 0$ means the roots are **non-real**! Always verify $\Delta \ge 0$ when roots are specified as real.
4. **Even-Multiplicity Zeros in Strict Inequalities ($<$ or $>$)**:
   - For $(x - 3)^2 (x - 5) < 0$, the solution is $(-\infty, 5) \setminus \{3\}$. Forgetting to puncture $x = 3$ changes the count of positive integer solutions from $\{1, 2, 4\}$ (3 solutions) to 4!

## 5. Active-Recall Drill (10 Prompts)

<details><summary>Q1: For what value(s) of k does the system kx + 3y = k - 3 and 12x + ky = k have no solution?</summary>
A: k/12 = 3/k != (k-3)/k. From k^2 = 36, k = 6 or k = -6. At k = 6, 6/12 = 3/6 = 3/6 (infinite solutions). At k = -6, -6/12 = 3/(-6) != -9/(-6) (no solution). Answer: k = -6 only.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2: How many positive integer solutions (x, y) exist for 5x + 9y = 180?</summary>
A: 5x is a multiple of 9 -> x = 9k. Since x >= 1 and y >= 1 (so 9y <= 175 -> 5x >= 5), x = 9k for 9k < 180/5 = 36 -> k in {1, 2, 3}. Thus 3 solutions.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3: Find the largest integer value that CANNOT be expressed as 7x + 13y for non-negative integers x, y.</summary>
A: By Frobenius Coin Theorem (gcd(7,13) = 1): ab - a - b = 7*13 - 7 - 13 = 91 - 20 = 71.
</details>

<details><summary>Q4: If alpha and beta are roots of x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0, find the quadratic equation whose roots are alpha^2 and beta^2.</summary>
A: Replace x with sqrt(x): (sqrt(x) - 3)^2? No, isolate sqrt(x): x + 3 = 5*sqrt(x) -> square both sides: (x + 3)^2 = 25x -> x^2 - 19x + 9 = 0.
</details>

<details><summary>Q5: Find the minimum value of f(x) = |x - 2| + |x - 5| + |x - 7| + |x - 12| and the range of x where it occurs.</summary>
A: Even number of terms (4) -> minimum occurs for all x in [5, 7]. Minimum value = (12 - 2) + (7 - 5) = 10 + 2 = 12.
</details>

<details><summary>Q6: How many integer pairs (x, y) satisfy |x| + |y| <= 4?</summary>
A: Formula 2r^2 + 2r + 1 for r = 4 gives 2(16) + 2(4) + 1 = 41 integer lattice points.
</details>

<details><summary>Q7: Solve |x^2 - 4x| < 5 for real x.</summary>
A: -5 < x^2 - 4x < 5. Left side: x^2 - 4x + 5 = (x - 2)^2 + 1 > 0 (always true for all real x). Right side: x^2 - 4x - 5 < 0 -> (x - 5)(x + 1) < 0 -> x in (-1, 5).
</details>

<details><summary>Q8: A polynomial P(x) leaves remainder 3 when divided by (x - 1) and remainder 7 when divided by (x - 3). Find the remainder when P(x) is divided by (x - 1)(x - 3).</summary>
A: Let remainder R(x) = ax + b. R(1) = a + b = 3 and R(3) = 3a + b = 7 -> 2a = 4 -> a = 2, b = 1. Remainder is 2x + 1.
</details>

<details><summary>Q9: Find the sum of all real roots of ||x - 4| - 5| = 3.</summary>
A: Since 0 < 3 < 5, the W-graph centered at x = 4 has 4 distinct real roots symmetric about x = 4. Sum of roots = 4 * 4 = 16.
</details>

<details><summary>Q10: For what range of k does x^2 - (k - 2)x + (k + 1) = 0 have one root strictly less than 2 and the other strictly greater than 2?</summary>
A: Since leading coefficient a = 1 > 0, condition is f(2) < 0 -> 4 - 2(k - 2) + k + 1 < 0 -> 9 - k < 0 -> k > 9.
</details>

#### CAT2025_S2_QA_Q07 (`CAT_2025_QA_Slot_2` | MCQ | Medium | `Round_1_Sitter`)
- **Sub-Topic**: Quadratic & Higher-Degree Polynomials — *Exponential quadratic substitution and Vieta product of roots*
- **Verified Answer**: `(D) 20` (Let u = 3^(x^2+2x-3). Then u^2 - 12u + 27 = 0 => u = 3 or u = 9. Thus x^2 + 2x - 3 = 1 (x^2 + 2x - 4 = 0, product of roots = -4) or x^2 + 2x - 3 = 2 (x^2 + 2x - 5 = 0, product of roots = -5). Product of all 4 real roots = (-4)*(-5) = 20.)
- **Mental Trigger (`See X -> Do Y`)**: Write 3^(x^2+2x-2) = 3 * 3^(x^2+2x-3) so the equation becomes u^2 - 12u + 27 = 0.
- **First-Principles Method**: Substitute u = 3^(x^2+2x-3) into 9^(x^2+2x-3) - 4*3^(x^2+2x-2) + 27 = 0 to get u^2 - 12u + 27 = 0 => (u - 3)(u - 9) = 0. Equating exponents gives x^2 + 2x - 4 = 0 (Delta = 20 > 0, root product = -4) and x^2 + 2x - 5 = 0 (Delta = 24 > 0, root product = -5). Total product = (-4)(-5) = 20.
- **30-Second Exam-Hall Speed Shortcut**: Once x^2 + 2x - 3 in {1, 2}, the constant terms of x^2 + 2x - (3+k) = 0 are -4 and -5; their product is (-4)(-5) = 20.
- **Trap Alert & Boundary Condition**: Always verify Discriminant > 0 for both quadratic branches before multiplying Vieta root products.
