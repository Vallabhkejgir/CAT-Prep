# VARC 02: RC Question Types, Critical Reasoning in RC & The 7 Deadly Distractor Traps

**Canonical Taxonomy Mapping**: `VARC > Reading Comprehension (RC) > RC Question Types & Option Elimination Matrix`, `The 7 Deadly RC Distractor Archetypes`  
**Core Rule**: **Never "select" the right answer in RC—always ELIMINATE the 3 flawed options using objective textual defects.**

---

## 1. The 7 Deadly RC Distractor Archetypes (Memorize & Spot in 5 Seconds)

Over 90% of wrong options in CAT RC are constructed using one of these 7 mechanical distortion templates:

| # | Distractor Archetype | How the Test-Maker Engineers It | Red-Flag Words / Detection Trigger |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Extreme Quantifier / Absolute Trap** | Replaces the passage's hedged claim (*often, can, contributes to*) with an absolute universal (*always, never, only, sole, impossible, completely, inevitably*). | `only`, `never`, `always`, `all`, `none`, `impossible`, `exclusively`, `the primary/main` (when unranked) |
| **2** | **Unauthorized / Fake Comparison** | Passage mentions both $X$ and $Y$ independently; the option invents a comparative ranking ($X > Y$, *more than*, *less effective than*, *faster than*). | `more than`, `less than`, `better`, `harder`, `preferable to`, `unlike X` (when not contrasted in text) |
| **3** | **Causal Reversal / Correlation-as-Causation** | Passage says $A$ correlates with $B$ (or $A \to B$); option states $B \to A$ or claims $A$ is the *sole cause* of $B$. | `causes`, `leads to`, `results from`, `because of`, `triggered by` |
| **4** | **Right Line, Wrong Question** | 100% factually true statement copied from Paragraph 3, but answers *what happened* when the question stem asked *why the author disagrees*. | Re-read the **exact question stem verb** before locking any "familiar-sounding" option! |
| **5** | **Half-Right, Half-Poisoned (Tail-End Twist)** | First 12 words match the passage's thesis beautifully; the **last 3 words** inject an unsupported adjective or false condition. | **Always read the final 4 words** of every option first when down to 2 choices! |
| **6** | **Scope Shift (Descriptive $\to$ Normative / Narrow $\to$ Broad)** | Passage describes what *is* happening (`descriptive`); option prescribes what governments *should/must* do (`normative`). Or shifts `some species` $\to$ `most animals`. | `should`, `must`, `ought to`, `needs to`; or widening `18th-century France` $\to$ `European history` |
| **7** | **Alien / Real-World Plausibility Trap** | Uses a statement that is true in real life (common sense / current affairs) but is **never mentioned or entailed** by the passage. | Ask: *"Can I put my finger on the sentence that proves this?"* If not, eliminate! |

## 2. Question-Type Execution Matrix

### 2.1 Main Idea / Primary Purpose / Best Title
- **What It Tests**: The overarching synthesis of **all paragraphs** ($P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + P_4$), centered on the **Author's Pivot**.
- **3 Classic Wrong Options**:
  1. **Too Narrow (Single-Paragraph Trap)**: Captures only $P_3$'s example or sub-point.
  2. **Too Broad**: Generalizes beyond the passage's specific subject.
  3. **Foil / Old-View Trap**: Summarizes the counter-argument introduced in $P_1$.

### 2.2 Inference / "Which of the following can be inferred?"
- **CAT Definition of Inference**: In CAT (unlike everyday guesswork), an inference is a **strict logical consequence / contrapositive / restatement** of what is explicitly stated in the text—**ZERO speculative leap allowed**.
- **Golden Rule**: Softer, hedged options (*"X may not always guarantee Y"*, *"Some forms of A can coexist with B"*) beat bold, sweeping claims 9 times out of 10 in Inference questions.

### 2.3 Critical Reasoning in RC (Strengthen, Weaken, Assumption, Paradox, Flaw)
Decompose the target argument into:
$$\text{Premise (Evidence)} + \text{Unstated Assumption} \implies \text{Conclusion (Claim)}$$

| CR Question Type | Exact Attack Algorithm |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Necessary Assumption** | Apply the **Negation Test**: Negate the option; if the author's conclusion **collapses**, that option is the necessary assumption! (Avoid options that merely bring in new tangential support.) |
| **Strengthen / Support** | Validate the unstated assumption, rule out an alternative explanation, or provide an example where `Cause present -> Effect present`. |
| **Weaken / Undermine / Challenge** | Attack the link between Premise and Conclusion: show an **alternative cause**, **reverse causation**, or a case where `Cause present -> Effect absent`. (Never attack the premise itself if the prompt accepts it as fact!) |
| **Resolve the Paradox / Discrepancy** | Find the option that allows **BOTH** seemingly contradictory facts ($A$ and $B$) to be true simultaneously. Eliminate any option that explains only $A$ or denies $B$. |
| **Parallel Reasoning / Analogy** | Match the **logical skeleton and certainty degree**, ignoring superficial topic similarity! |

## 3. Worked Exemplar: Dissecting a 4-Option CAT RC Trap

> **Passage Excerpt**: *"While algorithmic credit scoring has expanded loan access to previously unbanked borrowers who lack formal credit histories, its reliance on opaque behavioral proxies—such as smartphone battery habits or social network metadata—risks encoding historical socio-economic biases under a veneer of mathematical objectivity."*
>
> **Question**: Which of the following can be most reasonably inferred from the excerpt above?
> - **(A)** Algorithmic credit scoring is more biased against unbanked borrowers than traditional credit-history-based lending.
> - **(B)** Mathematical models used in credit scoring should be replaced with human loan officers to eliminate socio-economic bias.
> - **(C)** The use of non-traditional data in credit scoring can simultaneously broaden financial inclusion and perpetuate existing inequalities.
> - **(D)** Borrowers who maintain poor smartphone battery habits are universally denied loans by algorithmic scoring systems.

### 10-Second Elimination Breakdown
- **Option (A)**: **Trap #2 (Unauthorized Comparison)** — The excerpt never compares whether algorithmic scoring is *more* or *less* biased than traditional lending. *Eliminate!*
- **Option (B)**: **Trap #6 (Descriptive $\to$ Normative Scope Shift) + Trap #7 (Alien)** — "Should be replaced with human loan officers" is never suggested. *Eliminate!*
- **Option (D)**: **Trap #1 (Extreme Quantifier)** — "Universally denied" distorts "risks encoding biases". *Eliminate!*
- **Option (C)**: **Correct!** Maps 1-to-1 to the two clauses: *"broaden financial inclusion"* ("expanded loan access to previously unbanked borrowers") + *"perpetuate existing inequalities"* ("risks encoding historical socio-economic biases").

## 4. Double-Negative Question Stem Translation Rule

CAT frequently uses convoluted stems such as:
- *"All of the following, if true, would **undermine** the author's claim **EXCEPT**:"*
  - **Translation on Scratchpad**: Write `3 WEAKEN (Eliminate) | 1 STRENGTHENS or IRRELEVANT (Pick!)`.
- *"Which of the following is **NOT incompatible** with the author's argument?"*
  - **Translation**: `NOT incompatible` $=$ **Compatible / Consistent**!
- **Mark `+` or `-` next to each option (A, B, C, D)** on your scratchpad for every `EXCEPT / NOT` question so you never invert the logic at the final click.

## 5. Active-Recall Drills

<details><summary>Q1: You are stuck between Option A ('X often contributes to Y') and Option C ('X is the primary driver of Y') on an Inference question. The passage states 'X plays a significant role in shaping Y.' Which option do you eliminate and why?</summary>
A: Eliminate Option C! 'Significant role' does NOT mean 'primary (#1) driver' (Trap #1/#2: unsubstantiated superlative/ranking). Option A ('often contributes to') stays strictly within the passage's evidentiary bounds.
</details>

<details><summary>Q2: How do you apply the Negation Test to verify a 'Necessary Assumption' option?</summary>
A: Negate the option's main verb (e.g., 'Consumers do NOT notice price increases'). If negating the option destroys the author's conclusion, the option is a necessary assumption.
</details>

<details><summary>Q3: A passage explains how 19th-century railways standardized time zones across North America. An option in a Main Idea question states: 'Railways were the most important technological innovation of the 19th century.' Which two traps does this option commit?</summary>
A: Trap #1/2 (Extreme superlative / Unauthorized comparison: 'most important technological innovation') and Trap #6 (Too broad: ignores the specific focus on standardized time zones).
</details>

<details><summary>Q4: Translate the question stem: 'None of the following options fail to support the author's contention EXCEPT:'</summary>
A: 'Fail to support' = Do not support. 'None ... fail to support' = All of them DO support. '...EXCEPT' = Find the ONE option that DOES NOT support the author's contention!
</details>

<details><summary>Q5: In a Weaken question, the passage argues: 'City X installed 500 traffic cameras in January, and fatal accidents dropped by 30% in February; therefore, traffic cameras reduce fatal accidents.' What are the 3 standard ways an option can weaken this argument?</summary>
A: (1) Alternative cause (e.g., a severe blizzard in February reduced total traffic volume by 50%); (2) Baseline/data flaw (February always has 30% fewer accidents than January due to seasonal travel patterns); (3) Counter-evidence elsewhere.
</details>

## CAT 2025 PYQ Edge Examples & Newly Surfaced Patterns

### Newly Surfaced Sub-Topic Variants & Pattern Triggers (CAT 2025)
- **De-Nesting Double-Negative Stems (`CAT_2025_VARC_Slot_2` `Q03` & `Q18`)**: Stems like *'Which of the following, if false, would contradict the passage EXCEPT'* reduce via Boolean parity (`False -> Contradict` = `True`; `EXCEPT` = `False / Contradicts passage`). Write `+` / `-` next to each option before choosing.
- **Opposite-Concept / Opposite-Process Application Questions (`CAT_2025_VARC_Slot_1` `Q21` & `CAT_2025_VARC_Slot_3` `Q04`)**: Watch for stems asking for a scenario that is the *opposite* of a passage concept (e.g., *opposite of democratisation* or *non-utilitarian prioritarianism*).
- **Half-Right Label Swap & Unauthorized Comparison Traps (`CAT_2025_VARC_Slot_3` `Q04` & `Q18`)**: Eliminate options that attach a correct theory's label to the rival theory's definition, or turn *'not inconsequential'* into *'safer than'*.

#### CAT2025_S3_VARC_Q03 (`CAT_2025_VARC_Slot_3` | MCQ | Medium | `Round_1_Sitter`)

- **Verified Answer**: `(4) There is a principled way to decide which ethical framework applies to which class of cases, so the system can select the relevant starting points before deriving a recommendation.` (In physics, different incompatible theories describe specific domains (quarks/electrons vs galaxies), so physicists know which theory's postulates to use for a given domain.
