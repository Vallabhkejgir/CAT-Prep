# High-Recall Drill 04: VARC 2-Option Showdown Tie-Breaker (15 Showdowns)

> **Companion Reference**: [`../cheat_sheets/VARC_Elimination_and_VA_Rules.md`](../cheat_sheets/VARC_Elimination_and_VA_Rules.md) | [`../../varc_concept_examples/README.md`](../../varc_concept_examples/README.md) | [`README.md`](./README.md)

**Instructions**: In VARC, eliminating the first 2 options is easy; your percentile is decided when you are **down to the final 2 contenders**. For each showdown below, name the **exact mechanical defect** in the losing option before expanding the answer.

---

## Part A: Reading Comprehension (RC) 2-Option Showdowns (1–10)

<details><summary><b>Showdown 1 (Inference): Text says: "While algorithmic hiring filters screen thousands of resumes in seconds, their reliance on historical employee data often penalizes non-traditional career paths."
• Option A: Algorithmic hiring filters can perpetuate past workforce biases while accelerating resume screening.
• Option B: Algorithmic hiring filters are more biased against non-traditional applicants than human recruiters.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option A**
- **Defect in Option B**: **Trap #2 (Unauthorized Comparison)** — The text never compares algorithmic bias against *human recruiters*.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 2 (Main Idea): P1 states the traditional view that medieval guilds stifled innovation. P2–P4 show how guilds actually standardized apprenticeship training and lowered quality-verification costs.
• Option A: Medieval guilds played a complex role that both hindered and promoted technological innovation.
• Option B: Far from merely obstructing progress, medieval guilds fostered economic development through skill standardization and quality assurance.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option B**
- **Defect in Option A**: **Foil Equal-Weight Trap** — P1's "stifled innovation" was the *rejected old view*, not an equal half of the author's thesis!
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 3 (EXCEPT Question): Text says: "Subjective emotional attachment to a landscape is no less real than a cartographer's survey map; both literary and scientific records are needed in unison."
Which of the following is supported by the text EXCEPT:
• Option A: Neither a survey map nor a literary account alone provides an exhaustive record of a place.
• Option B: Emotional and spiritual experience of a landscape can replace a cartographer's survey map.</b></summary>

- **Winner (The Exception)**: **Option B**
- **Defect in Option B**: **Complementarity $\to$ Replacement Distortion** — *"No less real than / working in unison"* $\neq$ *"can replace"*.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 4 (Application): Text defines Utilitarianism as maximizing aggregate total wellbeing across a population. Which decision departs from Utilitarianism?
• Option A: Guided by deontological rights, the committee chose the flood-barrier plan that protected the largest total number of residents.
• Option B: The council followed a prioritarian rule, directing funds to the poorest ward rather than maximizing total city-wide output.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option B**
- **Defect in Option A**: **Trap #5 (Half-Right Label Swap / Tail-End Poison)** — Starts with *"deontological rights"*, but the action in the second half (*"protected the largest total number"*) is pure utilitarianism!
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 5 (Double-Negative Stem): Text says: "Unlike optical telescopes, radio astronomy revealed non-thermal cosmic phenomena invisible to the human eye."
"All of the following, if false, would contradict the text EXCEPT:"
• Option A: Radio astronomy detected cosmic phenomena that optical telescopes could not see.
• Option B: Optical telescopes were sufficient to observe all non-thermal cosmic phenomena.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option B**
- **Stem Translation**: `if false, contradicts EXCEPT` $=$ **Find the statement that is ALREADY FALSE in the text!** Option B directly contradicts the text.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 6 (Necessary Assumption): Text argues: "Because contact-tracing apps alert users to nearby infection hotspots, deploying the app will crowding-shift commuters onto alternative routes and inadvertently raise congestion risk."
• Option A: Commuters adjust their daily routes in response to app alerts and interact along shared alternative corridors.
• Option B: Contact-tracing Bluetooth sensors are 100% accurate within a two-meter radius at all times.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option A**
- **Defect in Option B**: **Trap #1 (Extreme Quantifier `100%... at all times`)** + fails the **Negation Test** (even with 80% accuracy, behavioral rerouting still causes crowding).
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 7 (4-Term Concept Mapping): Passage discusses (P1) 19th-century courtroom debates over criminal insanity, (P2) psychiatric diagnoses, (P3) moral responsibility, and (P4) sentencing/punishment.
• Option A: Judgement, Insanity, Punishment, Patriotism.
• Option B: Judgement, Insanity, Punishment, Responsibility.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option B**
- **Defect in Option A**: **Last-Word Poison** — *"Patriotism"* (4th word) is completely alien to the passage!
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 8 (Weaken): Text says: "Crossing eyed surface fish with eyeless cavefish produced some eyeless offspring with white fat and some with yellow fat; researchers therefore inferred no genetic link between eyelessness and yellow fat."
Which finding would INVALIDATE the researchers' inference?
• Option A: Some eyeless offspring had white fat.
• Option B: Only eyeless offspring had yellow fat.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option B**
- **Defect in Option A**: **Strengthen vs. Weaken Inversion** — Option A is the exact evidence cited *for* the researchers' inference, whereas Option B establishes a strict correlation that *invalidates* "no link"!
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 9 (Tone): Author critiques colonial forestry laws by citing archival records showing how timber quotas displaced village commons while claiming ecological conservation.
• Option A: Critical and analytical
• Option B: Derisive and polemical</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option A**
- **Defect in Option B**: **Tone Intensity Trap** — *"Derisive"* (mocking/ridiculing) overstates evidence-based archival critique.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 10 (Specific Detail): Text says: "While small run-of-river dams are far from inconsequential—impeding flow on two-thirds of major rivers—policy debates have focused disproportionately on mega-dams."
• Option A: Small dams have received less policy scrutiny than mega-dams despite their widespread river impact.
• Option B: Small run-of-river dams are environmentally safer than mega-dams.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option A**
- **Defect in Option B**: **Trap #2 (Unauthorized Comparison)** — *"safer than"* is never stated; *"not inconsequential"* actually emphasizes their harm!
</details>

## Part B: Verbal Ability (VA) 2-Option Showdowns (11–15)

<details><summary><b>Showdown 11 (Para-Summary): Paragraph explains that as immunity ages, declining apoptosis fails to clear damaged senescent ("zombie") cells, which secrete chemicals causing chronic inflammation and age-related diseases.
• Option A: Declining apoptosis during aging causes senescent zombie cells to accumulate, driving chronic inflammation and age-related diseases.
• Option B: As humans age, declining apoptosis allows senescent zombie cells to resist clearance and accumulate in body tissues.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option A**
- **Defect in Option B**: **Half-Summary Trap** — Stops halfway through the causal chain and completely omits *chronic inflammation and age-related diseases*!
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 12 (Para-Summary Agent Check): Text says: "Charles Darrow sold a simplified version of Elizabeth Magie's Landlord's Game to Parker Brothers. They paid Magie only $500 for her patent without royalties and credited Darrow as sole inventor."
• Option A: Charles Darrow plagiarized Magie's game and sold it to Parker Brothers for a meagre $500, denying her royalties.
• Option B: Parker Brothers acquired Darrow's simplified version of Magie's game and commercialized it while giving Magie minimal pay and recognition.</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Option B**
- **Defect in Option A**: **Agent-Action Swap** — *Parker Brothers* ("They") paid Magie $500 without royalties; Darrow did not sell it to Parker Brothers for a meagre $500!
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 13 (4-Slot Sentence Placement): Target Sentence S: "While taste is related to judgment, taste retains a vital link to gustatory pleasure and embodiment."
In the paragraph, Sentence 1 ends with "...highlight a neglected historical change. [Slot 2] Over the course of the 17th century, the word taste shifted from the palate to aesthetics. [Slot 3]"
• Contender 1: Place S at [Slot 2].
• Contender 2: Place S at [Slot 3].</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **Contender 2 (`[Slot 3]`)**
- **Defect in Contender 1**: **Breaks an Unbreakable Pair!** Sentence 1 ends with *"a neglected historical change"*, which MUST be followed immediately by *"Over the course of the 17th century..."* across `[Slot 2]`!
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 14 (Odd-One-Out): 5 sentences on deep-sea oxygen:
(1) Half of Earth's oxygen is made near the ocean surface by sunlit phytoplankton.
(2) Scientists discovered they also produce oxygen in complete darkness on the seafloor.
(4) This is surprising because oxygen is typically made by photosynthetic organisms, not seafloor rocks.
(5) Deep-sea rocks called polymetallic nodules don't only host sea critters.
Which sentence is the Odd One Out: (1) or (5)?</b></summary>

- **Winner (The Outlier)**: **Sentence (1)**
- **Defect in Eliminating (5)**: **Correlative Bridge + Pronoun Hijack!** `(5) "don't only host..." -> (2) "they also produce oxygen on the seafloor"` is an unbreakable grammatical pair where *"they"* = deep-sea rocks! Sentence (1) was planted to hijack *"they"* with *"phytoplankton"*.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Showdown 15 (Para-Jumble Final Swap): You have locked 2-4 ("Old books carry a scent..." -> "This familiar aroma is a result of chemical changes...") and 1-3 ("cellulose breaks down, releasing volatile organic compounds" -> "These compounds include vanillin").
• Contender A: 2413
• Contender B: 1324</b></summary>

- **Winner**: **`2413` (Contender A)**
- **Defect in `1324`**: **General-to-Specific Funnel Violation** — `2` introduces the broad phenomenon (*"Old books carry a scent"*), `4` attributes it to *"chemical changes"*, and `1-3` specifies the exact chemistry (*cellulose $\to$ volatile compounds $\to$ vanillin*).
</details>
