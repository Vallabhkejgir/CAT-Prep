# Last-Mile Rapid-Revision Cheat Sheet: VARC Elimination & VA Rules

Ultra-dense exam-hall execution guide for the 40-minute VARC section (**26 min for 4 RC Passages + 14 min for 8 VA Questions**).

---

## 1. The 4-Role RC Structural Reading Filter (3 Min / Passage)

| Signal Words in Passage | Structural Role | Exam Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Traditionally, Conventional wisdom, It is often assumed, Proponents of X claim...* | **The Foil / Old View** | Mentally tag `[NOT AUTHOR]`; wait for the pivot! |
| *However, Yet, But, Paradoxically, What this misses is, In reality, Nevertheless...* | **Author's Thesis Pivot** | **Lock this sentence!** It governs Main Idea & Primary Purpose. |
| *Admittedly, Granted, To be sure, While it is true that [X], [Y]...* | **Concession $\to$ Point** | $[X]$ is conceded; the author's true emphasis is $[Y]$ after the comma. |
| *For instance, Specifically, In a 2021 study, Consider...* | **Supporting Example** | Skim in 5 seconds; note only *which claim* it supports. |

## 2. The 7 Deadly RC Distractor Traps (The 2-Option Tie-Breaker)

When down to 2 options, scan both options for these 7 mechanical defects:
1. **Extreme Quantifier Trap**: Uses `always, never, only, sole, completely, impossible, all, primary` where the text used hedged language (`can, often, one of, suggests`).
2. **Unauthorized Comparison**: Uses `more than, less than, better, harder, unlike` between $X$ and $Y$ when the text never ranked them against each other.
3. **Causal Reversal / Fake Causation**: Flips $A \to B$ into $B \to A$, or turns correlation into causation.
4. **Right Line, Wrong Question**: True statement from Paragraph 2 that fails to answer the specific question stem.
5. **Tail-End Poison**: First 80% of the option is accurate; the **last 3–4 words** inject a false modifier or condition. *(Always check the last 4 words first!)*
6. **Scope / Normative Shift**: Shifts descriptive `is/was` to prescriptive `should/must/ought to`, or widens `some cases` to `most cases`.
7. **Alien Concept**: Plausible real-world statement not grounded in the passage.

## 3. Critical Reasoning in RC — 1-Line Attack Rules

- **Inference**: Must be $100\%$ entailed by the text (contrapositive or tight synthesis). Prefer **hedged/modest** options over sweeping claims.
- **Necessary Assumption**: Apply the **Negation Test** — negate the option; if the author's conclusion collapses, it's the correct answer.
- **Strengthen / Support**: Validates an unstated assumption, rules out an alternative cause, or shows `Cause -> Effect`.
- **Weaken / Undermine**: Introduces an alternative cause, reverses causation, or shows `Cause present -> Effect absent`.
- **`EXCEPT / NOT` Stems**: Write `+` (supports) or `-` (weakens) next to each option A, B, C, D on your scratchpad so you never invert the double negative.

## 4. Verbal Ability (VA) — TITA & MCQ Algorithms (14 Minutes)

### 4.1 Para-Jumbles (4-Sentence TITA)
1. **Find the Independent Opener**: Reject sentences starting with dangling pronouns (`This, These, Such, They, It`) or contrast/continuation connectors (`However, Moreover, Consequently, Still, Instead`).
2. **Lock 1–2 Mandatory Pairs**:
   - `Indefinite Noun ("a policy")` $\to$ `Definite/Demonstrative ("the policy / this measure")`
   - `Full Name/Title` $\to$ `Surname/Acronym`
   - `Claim/Phenomenon` $\to$ `"This paradox / Such an outcome"`
   - `Problem/Question` $\to$ `Resolution/Answer`
   - `Positive Claim` $\to$ `Pivot ("However / Yet") + Negative Claim`
3. **General-to-Specific Flow**: Broad Premise $\to$ Mechanism $\to$ Example/Implication.

### 4.2 Odd-One-Out (5-Sentence TITA)
- **Golden Rule**: **Build the 4-sentence Para-Jumble FIRST!** The leftover sentence is the outlier.
- **Trap Alert**: Never eliminate a sentence just because it uses a pronoun (`It/This`) instead of repeating the main keyword; the outlier sentence always repeats the main keyword while shifting **aspect, scale, tense, or thesis**.

### 4.3 Para-Summary (MCQ)
- **3-Point Filter**:
  1. **Complete**: Covers the author's main pivot/conclusion + core rationale (not just the opening setup).
  2. **Accurate**: No quantifier inflation (`some` $\to$ `all`) or fake comparisons.
  3. **Contained**: Excludes raw examples and unstated `should/must` policy advice.

### 4.4 Sentence Placement (`[1] [2] [3] [4]` MCQ)
1. Read the paragraph **without** the target sentence $S$ first to spot:
   - **Unbreakable adjacent pairs** (cross out those slots immediately!).
   - **The jarring gap / orphan pronoun** where a transition or antecedent is missing.
2. Verify both **Bridge In** (hooks into preceding sentence) and **Bridge Out** (sets up following sentence).
