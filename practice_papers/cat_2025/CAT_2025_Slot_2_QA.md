### Question 2 (`CAT2025_S2_QA_Q01` | MCQ | Easy | `Round_1_Sitter`)

Let $a_n$ be the $n^{\text{th}}$ term of a decreasing infinite geometric progression. If $a_1 + a_2 + a_3 = 52$ and $a_1 a_2 + a_2 a_3 + a_3 a_1 = 624$, then the sum of this geometric progression is:

- **(A)** $57$
- **(B)** $63$
- **(C)** $54$
- **(D)** $60$

<details><summary><b>Hint (Topic, Difficulty, Round Tag & Mental Trigger)</b></summary>

- **Classification**: `Algebra > Sequences & Series` | **Difficulty**: `Easy` | **Recommended Pass**: `Round_1_Sitter`
- **Core Concept Tested**: Three-term GP symmetric identity a1*a2 + a2*a3 + a3*a1 = a2*(a1 + a2 + a3)
- **Exam-Hall Mental Trigger (`See X -> Do Y`)**: For ANY 3 consecutive GP terms, a1*a2 + a2*a3 + a3*a1 = a2*(a1 + a2 + a3), so divide the two given sums immediately to get a2 = 624/52 = 12!

</details>

<details><summary><b>Verified Answer & Dual-Method Solution (First-Principles + 30s Shortcut + Trap Alert)</b></summary>

- **Verified Answer**: **`(C) 54`**
- **Quick Derivation Summary**: In GP a, ar, ar^2: a(1 + r + r^2) = 52 and a^2*r*(1 + r + r^2) = 624. Dividing gives a*r = 12. Then 12/r + 12 + 12r = 52 => 3r^2 - 10r + 3 = 0 => r = 1/3 (decreasing GP), a = 36. Sum = a/(1 - r) = 36/(2/3) = 54.
- **Method 1 — First-Principles Derivation**: Let the GP be a, ar, ar^2 with 0 < r < 1. Since a1*a2 + a2*a3 + a3*a1 = ar(a + ar + ar^2), we get ar = 624/52 = 12. Then a + ar^2 = 52 - 12 = 40 => 12/r + 12r = 40 => r = 1/3, a = 36. Infinite sum S = 36/(1 - 1/3) = 54.
- **Method 2 — 30-Second Exam-Hall Speed Shortcut**: Once a2 = 12 and a1 + a3 = 40, spot 36 + 4 = 40 with 36*4 = 12^2 => a1 = 36, r = 1/3 => S = 36 * (3/2) = 54.
- **Trap Alert & Boundary Condition**: Since the GP is decreasing, choose r = 1/3 (not r = 3, for which the infinite sum diverges).
- **Companion Insight References**: [`06_algebra_sequences_series_maxima_minima.md`](../../insights/topic_summaries/quant/06_algebra_sequences_series_maxima_minima.md), [`quant_pitfalls.md`](../../insights/mistakes_and_edge_cases/quant_pitfalls.md)

</details>

### Question 3 (`CAT2025_S2_QA_Q02` | TITA | Easy | `Round_1_Sitter`)

Two tangents drawn from a point $P$ touch a circle with center $O$ at points $Q$ and $R$. Points $A$ and $B$ lie on $PQ$ and $PR$, respectively, such that $AB$ is also a tangent to the same circle. If $\angle AOB = 50^\circ$, then $\angle APB$, in degrees, equals: *(TITA)*

<details><summary><b>Hint (Topic, Difficulty, Round Tag & Mental Trigger)</b></summary>

- **Classification**: `Geometry & Mensuration > Circles` | **Difficulty**: `Easy` | **Recommended Pass**: `Round_1_Sitter`
- **Core Concept Tested**: Third-tangent theorem for angle subtended at the center of a circle
- **Exam-Hall Mental Trigger (`See X -> Do Y`)**: When tangent segment AB intercepted between two tangents PQ and PR subtends angle AOB at center O, use angle AOB = 90 - (1/2) angle APB => angle APB = 180 - 2 * angle AOB.

</details>

<details><summary><b>Verified Answer & Dual-Method Solution (First-Principles + 30s Shortcut + Trap Alert)</b></summary>

- **Verified Answer**: **`80`**
- **Quick Derivation Summary**: Let AB touch the circle at M. Then OA bisects angle QOM and OB bisects angle ROM, so angle QOR = 2 * angle AOB = 100 deg. In quadrilateral PQOR, angle OQP = angle ORP = 90 deg, so angle APB = 180 - angle QOR = 80 deg.
- **Method 1 — First-Principles Derivation**: Let AB touch the circle at M. By tangent symmetry, triangle OQA is congruent to triangle OMA and triangle ORB is congruent to triangle OMB. Thus angle QOR = 2 * angle AOB = 100 deg. Since OQ perp PQ and OR perp PR, angle APB = 180 - 100 = 80 deg.
- **Method 2 — 30-Second Exam-Hall Speed Shortcut**: Apply the standard identity angle APB = 180 - 2(50) = 80 in 5 seconds.
- **Trap Alert & Boundary Condition**: Do not confuse angle AOB (50 deg) with angle QOR (100 deg); taking 180 - 50 = 130 is the classic trap.
- **Companion Insight References**: [`07_geometry_triangles_circles_polygons.md`](../../insights/topic_summaries/quant/07_geometry_triangles_circles_polygons.md), [`quant_pitfalls.md`](../../insights/mistakes_and_edge_cases/quant_pitfalls.md)

</details>

### Question 4 (`CAT2025_S2_QA_Q03` | MCQ | Medium | `Round_1_Sitter`)

Let $ABCDEF$ be a regular hexagon and $P$ and $Q$ be the midpoints of $AB$ and $CD$, respectively. Then, the ratio of the areas of trapezium $PBCQ$ and hexagon $ABCDEF$ is:

- **(A)** $6 : 19$
- **(B)** $5 : 24$
- **(C)** $6 : 25$
- **(D)** $7 : 24$

<details><summary><b>Hint (Topic, Difficulty, Round Tag & Mental Trigger)</b></summary>

- **Classification**: `Geometry & Mensuration > Quadrilaterals & Polygons` | **Difficulty**: `Medium` | **Recommended Pass**: `Round_1_Sitter`
- **Core Concept Tested**: Regular hexagon decomposition into equilateral triangles and midline scaling
- **Exam-Hall Mental Trigger (`See X -> Do Y`)**: In a regular hexagon of side 2a, the long diagonal CF has length 4a; since P and Q are midpoints of AB and CD, PQ is the mid-segment of trapezium ABCF, so PQ = (2a + 4a)/2 = 3a!

</details>

<details><summary><b>Verified Answer & Dual-Method Solution (First-Principles + 30s Shortcut + Trap Alert)</b></summary>

- **Verified Answer**: **`(B) 5 : 24`**
- **Quick Derivation Summary**: Set regular hexagon side = 2. Area(ABCDEF) = 6 * sqrt(3). In trapezium PBCQ, BC = 2, PB = QC = 1, interior angles at B and C are 120 deg, so PQ = 2 + 2*(1*cos 60) = 3 and height h = 1*sin 60 = sqrt(3)/2. Area(PBCQ) = 0.5*(2 + 3)*(sqrt(3)/2) = (5/4)*sqrt(3). Ratio = (5/4) : 6 = 5 : 24.
